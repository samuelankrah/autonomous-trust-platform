The payments report request

A workload asks to read a protected report. The system checks who it is, what it is allowed to do, and whether its proof is fresh, then decides and enforces the decision.

Label for the story and the parts list: Conceptual explanation

Explore: the story in plain words

Label: Conceptual explanation

Think of a car arriving at a workshop. The workload is the driver arriving with a job to do. The identity is the driver proving who they are. The grant is the written authorization from the manager, naming what this driver may do. The attestation evidence is a recent inspection note for the vehicle, with a time on it. The decision point is the service desk that checks all three together and issues a signed work order. The enforcement point is the bay gate that checks the work order before letting the vehicle through. The protected resource is the bay and the report inside it. The evidence ledger is the workshop logbook where every check and outcome is written down in order.

Limits of this analogy: a car analogy does not capture signing, binding, or revocation checking, and the journey says so rather than stretching it.

Bridge note: the analogy is a memory aid only. In the actual model, the grant is not a simple note from a manager: it carries scoped delegated authority, a bounded permission from an authority source, for this resource and action. The enforcement point does not merely glance at a work order: it checks a cryptographically protected decision that is bound to this specific request.

Suggested order: revoke the grant, age the attestation, tamper with a decision, replay a decision, attempt a bypass. Free choice is allowed: pick any one of the five.

What it changes: Adds the current grant to the seeded revocation list. The grant document still exists.

What it changes: Advances the lab clock so the attestation age exceeds 300 seconds (the scenario uses 301 seconds), while identity and grant are issued fresh. Only the attestation becomes stale. The 300 second value is a synthetic lab constant, not a rule for real systems.

What it changes: Alters one claim in the signed decision after signing (the resource field, as in the engine scenario). It changes the decision text, not the signature.

What it changes: Takes a genuine decision bound to a first request and presents it for a second, different request, with a new request identifier and a new one-time value.

What it changes: Tries to reach the protected resource directly, with no decision at all.

One fault per run in every mode, guided and free choice alike. Combinations are unavailable, because the engine and the scenario contract define isolated single-fault scenarios only.

Label: Not modeled There is no control for credential rotation, no control for editing policy, and no control for a dependency outage, because the engine does not perform them.

        Repair and reset

        Diagnosis

        What broke, which stage caught it, and what will you change to repair it? All three answers are required before repair controls unlock.

        Repair

        Repair never broadens authority. A repair restores the happy path permissions exactly, it never adds a new permission, a wider resource, or a different action.

        For replay, the rerun is the normal path for a fresh second request: a fresh decision is obtained for the new request through the normal decision step and enforced for that request. The original decision stays bound to its own request, and the replay rejection remains recorded as evidence.

        Reset

        Reset will archive the current run and clear the active run, including all faults, predictions, and trace for the active run. The archived run stays reachable only through the Prior run view. Do you want to reset?

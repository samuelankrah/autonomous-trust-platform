The Gatehouse

You are the gatehouse apprentice, learning the Steward's craft from Steward Decia. Worlan, envoy of the Counting House, has come to fetch the King's Ledger from the Ledger Vault. Before any door opens, who he claims to be, what he is allowed to do, and how fresh his inspection is are all read and checked. Then Decia decides, and Captain Dorian acts on her decision. Nothing opens on trust.

Explore: the story in plain words

Label: Conceptual explanation

The court, in plain words:

  • Worlan, envoy of the Counting House (the workload): he does the asking. He has come for the King's Ledger.
  • Ideon the herald and the house colors (the identity): Ideon announces who Worlan claims to serve.
  • The Sealed Letter from Lord Grantham (the grant): this envoy may collect this one ledger, nothing else.
  • Quartermaster Atesia's inspection (attestation): her dated report on Worlan's party: gear sound, nothing messed with.
  • Steward Decia (the decision point): she reads the claim, the letter, and the inspection together, and decides.
  • Captain Dorian of the gate (the enforcement point): he opens the vault door only on Decia's signed order.
  • The Ledger Vault (the protected resource): where the King's Ledger is kept.
  • The Chronicle, kept by Brother Evidan (the evidence ledger): every arrival, check, decision, and outcome, written in order.
Where the kingdom story stops working

The story is a memory aid only, and it has limits. Real systems have no single wall or gate: requests arrive from everywhere at once.

Identity in real systems is proven with cryptographic keys, not faces, voices, or colors. Many checkers and guards act at the same time, in different places, and no person is doing the judging: software decides in a blink, by rules.

And the Sealed Letter in the real model is data, not paper: a grant carrying a bounded permission from an authority, for this resource and this action only. Captain Dorian's check is likewise more than a glance: he verifies a signed order that is protected against changes and tied to this one errand.

          Set it right

          Diagnosis

          What went wrong, which stage caught it, and what will you change to set it right? All three answers are required before the repair controls unlock.

          Repair

          Setting it right never widens permission. It restores the normal errand exactly: it never adds a new permission, a different ledger, or a different action.

          For Yesterday's Pass, the rerun is the normal path for a fresh second errand: a fresh order is obtained for the new errand through the normal decision step and enforced for that errand. The original order stays bound to its own errand, and the rejection remains recorded in the Chronicle.
